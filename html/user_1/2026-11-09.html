  • Subjects: cs.LG
  • Abstract URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09758
  • Pdf URL: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.09758
  • Copy Paste: [[2611.09758]] Diffusion-free Score Matching for Exact Conditional Sampling(https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09758)
  • Keywords: diffusion, generative
  • Abstract: The success of modern score-based generative models is largely driven by diffusion-based training, which learns a collection of time-indexed score fields corresponding to progressively noisier versions of the data distribution. While such models achieve impressive sample quality, leveraging them for sound probabilistic inference---particularly sampling from arbitrary conditionals of the model---remains challenging. This difficulty arises because conditioning information is only available for clean data and not for the higher noise levels required to generate exact conditional samples. In this paper, we introduce DIffusion-free SCOre matching (DISCO), a principled approach that sidesteps the need for time-dependent score fields altogether. Our method is based on an objective that, while reminiscent of diffusion-based training, estimates only a single score of the (slightly perturbed) data distribution, for which the exact conditional score is readily obtained. In experiments, DISCO is competitive with state-of-the-art diffusion models in terms of sample quality. More importantly, DISCO yields a more faithful representation of the underlying data distribution and---crucially---enables accurate sampling from arbitrary conditionals, outperforming standard heuristic samplers. This capability opens the door to principled and flexible probabilistic reasoning with score-based models.

Title: AR-Edit: Training-Free Autoregressive Video Editing without Inversion

  • Authors: Hovhannes Margaryan, Quentin Bammey, Christian Sandor, Vicky Kalogeiton
  • Subjects: cs.CV
  • Abstract URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09795
  • Pdf URL: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.09795
  • Copy Paste: [[2611.09795]] AR-Edit: Training-Free Autoregressive Video Editing without Inversion(https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09795)
  • Keywords: diffusion
  • Abstract: Editing a video stream in real time, without access to future frames, is essential for interactive applications. Existing training-free video editing methods, however, assume full offline access to the entire sequence, while streaming approaches either rely on additional training or are limited to specific editing techniques. We show that this limitation can be removed by analyzing the self-attention dynamics of DMD-distilled autoregressive (AR) video diffusion models. We uncover a key asymmetry: queries and keys remain stable and aligned with their source counterparts across denoising timesteps, while values diverge and encode the evolving content. Inherited from the non-distilled bidirectional teacher, this asymmetry is strongest at high noise, where few-step streaming models operate. The resulting mechanism is simple: a single forward pass suffices to capture source identity in the value features, which can be reused throughout generation. Starting from pure Gaussian noise and injecting only these source values reconstructs the input with fidelity matching or surpassing inversion-based methods, effectively eliminating inversion. Based on this insight, we introduce AR-Edit, a training-free, inversion-free method that caches source values once and selectively injects them during denoising. Across multiple AR backbones, AR-Edit enables real-time streaming editing with superior source preservation and tonal fidelity at minimal overhead.

Title: The Cost of Coverage: Conformalising Native Forecast Uncertainty for GB Wind Power

  • Authors: Carl Somers
  • Subjects: cs.LG, stat.AP
  • Abstract URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09815
  • Pdf URL: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.09815
  • Copy Paste: [[2611.09815]] The Cost of Coverage: Conformalising Native Forecast Uncertainty for GB Wind Power(https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09815)
  • Keywords: foundation model
  • Abstract: Uncertainty bands on wind forecasts help inform reserve sizing and commitments, but often fail to deliver their stated coverage. We apply conformal prediction to native bands to see what it adds and what it costs, comparing six forecasters, from ARIMA to a tabular foundation model, at two wind farms in Great Britain (GB) under operational conditions. We show that calibration repairs empirical marginal coverage with little or no Winkler Score penalty, but this apparent success conceals under-coverage in high winds. Calibrating by forecast wind regime turns marginal coverage into regime-level coverage, cutting day-ahead out-of-band volume in high winds by over 40% relative to pooled calibration while narrowing bands overall.

Title: ZeoBench: A Benchmark for Self-Supervised Learning on 3D Zeolite Representations

  • Authors: Aaron Sun, Yachan Liu, Ping Yang, Peng Bai, Subhransu Maji
  • Subjects: cs.CV
  • Abstract URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09880
  • Pdf URL: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.09880
  • Copy Paste: [[2611.09880]] ZeoBench: A Benchmark for Self-Supervised Learning on 3D Zeolite Representations(https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09880)
  • Keywords: self-supervised
  • Abstract: Zeolites are an important class of porous crystalline materials with broad applications in gas storage, separations, and catalysis. Although hundreds of thousands of experimentally realized and hypothetical zeolite structures are known, identifying materials optimized for a target application remains challenging because property labels are expensive to obtain and available only for limited structure-molecule pairs. While prior work has demonstrated that 3D neural representations can predict zeolite properties effectively, it remains unclear which representation-learning strategies are most effective in the low-data regime relevant to materials discovery. We present ZeoBench, a benchmark for self-supervised learning on dense 3D representations of all-silica zeolites, and evaluate the resulting features on six existing downstream adsorption-property prediction tasks, across a range of training set sizes. Our study compares self-supervised 3D pretraining on volumetric zeolite data, adaptations of image-pretrained 2D ConvNets and vision transformers to 3D via multiview and channel-inflation strategies, and 3D ConvNets pretrained on out-of-domain volumetric datasets, alongside hand-crafted descriptors and supervised baselines. Using our pretraining and evaluation framework, we find self-supervised 3D representations outperform existing methods and achieve state-of-the-art performance across both in-domain label regimes and for the unseen domain of metal organic frameworks. Learned representations consistently outperform hand-crafted descriptors, and convolutional architectures are generally more effective than vision transformers in this setting. These results establish ZeoBench as a practical benchmark for 3D representation learning in zeolites and provide guidance for data-limited adsorption property prediction.

Title: FlowMoP: Stochastic Multi-Person Motion Prediction

  • Abstract URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09882
  • Pdf URL: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.09882

  • Keywords: generative
  • Abstract: Predicting the future motion of multiple people is inherently stochastic: the same observed history admits many plausible continuations depending on intent, coordination, and social context. Yet the dominant paradigm in multi-person 3D motion prediction remains deterministic, with existing methods producing a fixed number of outputs per agent. In contrast, we present a conditional flow matching framework that jointly generates diverse future motions for multiple agents, modeling both global trajectories and root-relative poses. A B-spline reparameterization compresses the generative state space while enforcing temporal smoothness, and a dual-stream transformer conditions each agent's predictions on observed neighbor motion for interaction-aware forecasting. Our method achieves state-of-the-art performance on CMU-Mocap (UMPM) and 3DPW, outperforming deterministic baselines on joint position error, pose error, and final displacement error despite producing a full predictive distribution. We additionally establish the first motion prediction baseline on WorldPose, a large-scale professional soccer dataset, where social conditioning demonstrably concentrates predictive uncertainty along interaction-constrained directions.
